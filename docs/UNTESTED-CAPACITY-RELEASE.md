# Making room for an earned method

This is supplied opportunity-cost arbitration, not a learned claim that unknown
material is useless, safe, or nutritionally inferior. The actor must already have
its own paid acquisition, sampling and recognized sustained-benefit proof for
the intended method. Existing appetite/energy, care, availability and food-learning
ablation gates remain in charge.

When full, the existing personally adverse release remains first. An opt-in
fallback can instead select one personally acquired, still-held, untested item.
It requires four valid distinct held identities, paid acquisition proof, a
complete ordered receipt tail through the current memory counter, and no later
handling apart from inspection. Any retained sample, pending marker, or effect
on that item vetoes this fallback. Unlinked handling and ambiguous custody fail
closed. Adverse evidence that cannot authorize its own release does not authorize
substituting an unknown possession.

Release requires a fresh firsthand view of an earned source within reach. While
distant, the actor may carry everything along its existing personally observed
route and reconsider at each decision. This creates no persistent run state and
never discards en route. Missing routes or blocked sources do not authorize
release. The ordinary physical release still costs time and energy, leaves the
item in the world, and is followed by a fresh method selection.

Tests use bounded authored encounters, supplied positions/appetite, and actual
paid actions/delayed bodily feedback. They cover protected trials, custody loss,
malformed proof, care interruption, route/availability gates and current save
roundtrip. This is neither ordinary autonomous adoption nor survival evidence;
those require separately reported ordinary observations. Native persistence and
aggregate checks are separate release gates.

## Bounded ordinary development observations

Two fixed 600-second paired development cases used unchanged worlds, actor RNGs,
and initial physiology. They were diagnostic cases, not a held-out cohort.
In one case, the new choice actually paid for local release, repeated a personally
earned acquisition method, sampled its product and recognized sustained relief.
That actor's calorie deficit decreased by 30.715 kcal; the other actor's deficit
increased by 6.237 kcal through changed shared-object availability and optional
exploration. Both had one additional invalid action. The second case's full
receipt sequence and final actors were identical to baseline, with no capacity
choice. All four actor outcomes still had large calorie deficits. These results
support the narrow retrieval opportunity, not a general energy or survival claim.
