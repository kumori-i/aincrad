# Bounded Ozark walking and wading pilot

This pilot is an opt-in, fresh, **unsaved** walking session over one corridor and a short shallow-water reach of the approved Ozark landform. It uses the authored continuous sampler through the separate `ozark-wading-pilot-v2` identity. The existing offline preview remains nonplayable, and ordinary startup still selects the historical valley. A first integration slice places one fresh discovery-mode Aster at a dry river bank in this session.

## Entry and isolation

The desktop accepts the pilot only with `--test --test-profile=<disposable directory> --test-ozark-pilot` in an unpackaged build. This selects the exact `?test=1&ozarkPilot=1` entry route. The route constructs its own in-memory player, clock, and one fresh Aster using the existing discovery controller; it never loads the ordinary game boot, its save manager, ecology, or saved world. The pilot displays its unsaved status and discards progress on close. Neither the pilot identity nor the preview identity is accepted by the ordinary `freshState` or save path.

The center of the pilot corridor is part of the same 400-pixel-cell coordinate transform as the approved 96 by 72 Ozark descriptor. The walking rectangle is x = -1500..600 and y = -900..900 logical pixels. The fixed spawn is (-680, 380), with a ten-pixel actor radius and a checked dry route eastward to x = 0. Every movement step checks the actor's swept footprint against sampled water, the internal pond, corridor bounds, and local terrain grade. Axis sliding uses those same checks. The sampled sinkhole pond lies outside this short corridor; it is classified as a hazard, but this trial does not offer a route to it.

Only the selected main-channel reach at x = -1280..-1120, y = 180..390 can be entered. A radius-aware footprint-depth proxy at most 0.5 permits shallow wading; deeper water, all other channel water, and the pond remain blocked. The proxy comes from the authored channel's signed surface distance over a 100-pixel span. It is not measured depth or bathymetry. Wading slows walking to 65% of its dry rate. A small modeled downstream push follows consecutive validated main-channel cells 44 to 45; it is an authored flow cue, not a hydrodynamic calculation or real-world current estimate. Both walking and the push use the same swept collision checks. Internal 1/120-second slices keep the result stable when elapsed time is partitioned differently.

Player wetness and effort are bounded, transient session cues. They increase during shallow contact and recover on dry ground. Aster's shallow contact changes their actual thermal wetness and physical exertion stores through the existing biology path. Neither is written to a save. The pilot still has no swimming, drowning, persistent agents, learned wading skill, or full-map activation.

Each attempted move also produces a transient, first-person action receipt: attempt time, requested direction and duration, own start/end positions and displacement, locally observed footing and flow cues before/after, and qualitative apparent resistance, effort change, and wetness change. Progress is judged from the actor's observed displacement along the requested direction relative to its attempted gait. A near-stall is reported as negligible progress, without attributing it to a hidden collision or separating current drift from intentional motion. Session time continues through idle drift, making the latest receipt's age visible instead of presenting it as a fresh action. The receipt contains no depth proxy, global current map, collision table, route hint, or learned technique. It is an observation interface for future work, not an autonomous controller or evidence of learned behavior.

The grade rule is provisional and local to this experiment: the largest four-direction height change at an actor-sized probe must be at most 0.03 *relative height units per logical pixel*. The approved spawn-to-east route measures about 0.021, while a dry northwest patch near (-1450, -850) measures about 0.039. These are sampler units, not surveyed slopes or a general climbing skill. The line-of-sight check samples the same elevation field against a three-unit eye height. Local observations expose qualitative ground and habitat within visible pilot bounds, without hidden sampler numbers.

The browser constructor checks the fixed descriptor seed and dimensions but does not recompute its SHA-256 digest at runtime. The required `ozark-preview-pixels` test regenerates the same source descriptor and pins its sampler-relevant digest; the pilot test pins the separate policy identity. A source or policy change requires those tests to pass with a reviewed new identity before publication.

The renderer colors each terrain tile from this pilot's own sampled landform, using the same coordinate transform as collision and sight. The bounded shallow band has a lighter tint and an arrow along the authored downstream cue; deeper and unassessed water remain visually distinct. A splash marks player foot contact even where their center is on dry paint. Dry pixels blocked by the pilot grade policy retain their striped rock cue. It draws the player and Aster, with a camera toggle to watch Aster. It has no physical swimming, caves, food, ecology, or persistent full-map play.

## First agent slice and acceptance gates

Aster begins at the authored dry bank (-1200, 400) with seed 6 and empty skills, values, experiments, action experience, and tile memory. This bank and seed were selected as a deterministic demonstration fixture: the existing generic exploration choice reaches a water tile first. The destination is not supplied to the actor or special-cased in discovery policy. Other starts or seeds can lead to different choices, including dry exploration or blocked attempts; this slice does not establish a general river-seeking ability.

`createNpc` creates the fresh actor; the runtime Ozark binding checks the dry spawn. The first `NpcController` observation calls `perceive`, which samples only cells within local sight and line of sight. The actor remembers water as `surface-water` with an **attemptable** route flag. The renderer shows a lighter authored shallow band and current arrow to the player, but neither that band nor depth, current, grade, or physical `classify` results enter Aster's tile memory. Seen deep water has the same route flag, so a remembered tile is a proposed path, not a safety guarantee. The existing `discoveryDecision` scores observed unvisited cells without water preference; `knownRoute` uses only those remembered cells and failed edges. `requestNpcAction` admits only observed tile centers, then the NPC movement action calls the same `geometry.advance` as the player pilot. Swept physical collision can reject an attempted deep-water route and record the failure. That geometry also applies current drift when Aster is idle or unconscious; recovery searches Ozark walkability.

A completed move records local progress, resistance, footing, wetness change, and effort change in the bounded in-session experience ledger; the body changes during the action. The first route is an autonomous choice from locally observed candidate tiles in this seeded fixture, followed by a causal physical result. The choice is not evidence that Aster learned how to find safe water. A controlled comparison of later choices remains necessary.

The additional acceptance gates are distinct. A controlled, ablated comparison must show a later choice changed by the agent's own wading outcomes before claiming learned wading behavior. A versioned playable Ozark identity, map-aware save/load, and exact action/effect continuity across write/load must pass before claiming persistence. This first slice leaves both gates pending and retains the pilot's unsaved boundary.

## Verification

Focused tests check the identity gate, radius-aware dry route, shallow entry and exit, downstream displacement, deep water and pond contact, boundaries and excessive grade, timestep stability, sight/observation behavior, renderer sampling, and pilot entry isolation. The first-agent checks also compare choices after changing only a water label, reject a sight-blocked tile, bar geometry queries during decision, and show an observed deep-water attempt ending at the physical boundary. `npm run check` and `npm run build` verify the repository. `node scripts/verify-ozark-pilot-desktop.mjs` launches the real Electron application in a disposable test profile, sends native input through the dry bank and shallow reach, captures dry and shallow screenshots in `test-results/ozark-pilot-desktop/`, asserts zero pilot storage writes, and checks exact legacy save bytes after closing and reopening the profile. No pilot run should use a live profile or saved world.

`node scripts/verify-ozark-causal-observation.mjs` is a deterministic, headless scripted probe. It compares paired dry and shallow-water attempts, records entry and exit, and observes little forward progress on a northward attempt despite some actual movement. Its comparisons consume only first-person receipts and local observations, and it traps storage/profile access. A focused test separately checks that the fixture faces deep water; that sampler check is not an input to the probe. The script demonstrates an evidence interface, not learning.
