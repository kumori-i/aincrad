# Offline Ozark geometry sampler contract

Status: design preview only. This contract does not select a playable world, change a save, or give an agent a map.

## Input and coordinates

`createOzarkPreviewSampler(descriptor)` accepts a bounded version 1 descriptor from `generateOzarkPreview`. `sample(x, y)` uses relative macro-cell coordinates: an integer pair is the center of that descriptor cell, with valid bounds `0 <= x <= width - 1` and `0 <= y <= height - 1`. It rejects nonfinite and out-of-bounds input. There is deliberately no conversion to Aincrad's logical pixels, travel time, or surveyed elevation.

The sampler returns a fresh record containing:

- `elevation`: a continuous relative-height land surface. It starts with bilinear interpolation of the four surrounding descriptor cells and adds a compact crest along explicit watershed edges. `crestRise` is the nonnegative added height in relative units. The crest is zero at every integer cell center, preserving the approved source heights; its vertical units are not calibrated to playable slope limits. `moisture`, `canopy`, and `light` remain bilinear site scores.
- `habitat` and `substrate`: the nearest cell's site labels, with ties assigned toward the lower index. Categorical labels may change at boundaries; they are hypotheses about site character, not established plants, food, safety, or traversability.
- `drainage`: `surface` or `internal` according to the nearest lattice cell, with `terminalCell` pointing to that cell class's outlet or explicit internal sinkhole. Orthogonal neighbors of different classes define half-cell watershed edges. A query exactly on an edge returns `divide` and `terminalCell: null`; `watershedDistance` is its nonnegative distance to the nearest edge in relative cells, or `null` if no internal basin exists. Assigning a side is graph ownership, not an off-grid flow vector. No water receiver crosses an edge.
- `surfaceWaterDistance`: continuous signed distance in relative cells to the authored main river, tributaries, spring outflow, and spring pool footprints. Negative means inside a *drawn preview footprint*, not known water depth or a movement rule. `nearestChannel` is a fresh `{kind, id}` record, with `kind` one of `main`, `tributary`, `spring-outflow`, or `spring-pool`. Equal signed distances resolve in that order, with tributaries in descriptor order.
- `sinkholePondDistance`: continuous signed radial distance to the separately marked internal pond footprint, or `null` for seeds without a sinkhole. It is never merged with surface water.

For reproducibility, the provisional preview footprints use radii of `0.5` cell for the main river, `0.35` for tributaries, `0.28` for the spring outflow, `0.75` for its pool, and `sqrt(3)` for the sinkhole pond. These widths are drawing parameters, not measured discharge or safe crossing widths.

## Invariants and limits

The constructor rejects malformed dimensions, cells, paths, and termini. Queries are deterministic, bounded, and do not mutate the descriptor or any application state. At every lattice center, elevation, drainage, and habitat agree with the descriptor. Surface and internal centers retain separate terminals. Surface channel branches meet the main channel; the sinkhole has no surface outlet. The actual preview terrain is a continuous 2.5D relative-height field, with a narrow crest above both neighboring cell centers along every watershed edge. The former one-cell-wide mixed-class `divide` band was classification ambiguity, not a physical ridge. The new crest is a modeled landform, but relative heights alone do not establish climbability or every off-grid runoff direction. The source graph proves downhill flow at its cell receivers; no flow vector, water depth, crossing ability, plant stock, or resource promise is returned.

The channel's long straight and angular stretches originate in the authored model: its rounded, eastward cell path permits only horizontal or diagonal steps, and the SVG connects those points. A continuous query over that polyline does not make it a natural meander. A later geometry revision should define one smoother centerline used consistently by drawing, elevation, and drainage, then re-prove terminal and downhill properties. Smoothing the SVG alone would conceal the model limit.

## Offline integration fixtures and remaining gates

`scripts/ozark-preview-pixels.mjs` supplies a **pure offline pixel adapter**, without selecting this geometry for a playable or saved world. To use it, call `createOzarkPreviewPixelProfile({seed, centerX, centerY, cellPixels})`, then explicitly pass that profile to `createOzarkPreviewPixelAdapter(profile)`. Both functions regenerate and validate the authored descriptor for that unsigned 32-bit seed. There is no caller-supplied descriptor, legacy-world fallback, default profile, save field, or production import.

The transform uses uniform square macro cells. `(0,0)` and `(width-1,height-1)` denote opposite *sample-center* corners; `centerX,centerY` is their midpoint in logical pixels, and `cellPixels` is a positive integer. The y axis increases downward, as it does in `world.js`. Both corners must fit inside the current `[-24000,24000]` logical-pixel envelope. For an illustrative `centerX:0, centerY:0, cellPixels:400`, the 96 × 72 descriptor's sample-center rectangle is `[-19000,19000] × [-14200,14200]` pixels. This is a provisional coordinate frame, not an approved world scale, playable boundary, or guarantee that the remaining space is traversable. The SVG's nine-pixel drawing cell is unrelated. `relativeToPixel`, `pixelToRelative`, and `sampleAtPixel` reject nonfinite or out-of-bounds queries instead of clamping or wrapping. Distances in `sampleAtPixel` results remain in relative macro cells.

The profile's `identity` is a SHA-256 digest of the profile namespace, offline scope, sampler contract version, a digest of sampler-relevant generated geometry, and the exact transform. It distinguishes this preview from the current legacy map even when both use seed 74193. Adapter construction regenerates the descriptor and verifies every supplied profile field and digest. It snapshots the validated profile, so a caller changing its original object cannot redirect later queries. Changes to sampled geometry or sampler semantics require a new identity; update the explicit sampler contract version whenever interpolation, feature footprints, or categorical rules change. Cosmetic SVG and legend wording do not enter this identity.

`src/geometry-profile.js` now recognizes the exact approved offline identity through an explicit internal fresh-world request. Its result is marked nonplayable. `freshState({geometryProfile})` refuses that selection before constructing a world, while its ordinary no-option path keeps legacy geometry; an explicitly undefined request also fails. The running app has no selection UI or request. A save containing either reserved top-level selector, `geometryProfile` or `geometryIdentity`, is visibly protected without rewriting its bytes, so an explicit geometry identity cannot be loaded as legacy and erased on the next save. Unrelated unknown top-level fields retain the existing normalization behavior. No version 8 save stores this preview identity. This is an identity gate, not a new saved-world schema.

`src/preview-ground-dispatch.js` binds the approved offline adapter to a read-only ground source. The optional source argument to `groundColor` and `makeGroundChunk` renders its habitat and drawn water footprints without legacy map details. `node scripts/render-ozark-ground-fixture.mjs` writes a local terrain-only PNG in `test-results/ozark-preview/` through production `groundColor`; tests also exercise the chunk path. This diagnostic uses simplified surface-water and pond colors rather than the labels and layered styling of the approved SVG. Its amber band marks proximity within half a cell of the watershed; it is not a collision wall. The running renderer still uses legacy geometry.

`src/preview-traversal-fixture.js` binds that same approved sampler to provisional footprint-clearance checks. It distinguishes dry candidates from drawn surface channels, the separate internal pond, and preview bounds at an actor radius; selects the first dry candidate from an explicit list; and checks short straight segments with a water-distance margin between samples. A dry watershed crossing is not blocked merely because it changes drainage ownership. Results record watershed contact and leave topographic passability unverified. The crest is real relative-height geometry, but its slope has no calibrated rise/run or actor climbing rule. These are offline spawn and route diagnostics, not authoritative `world.js` collision or movement. The fixture adds no bridges, water transport, resources, caves, objects, sight, actor motion, saved geometry, or playable spawn.

`src/save.js` version 8 restores positions against current `world.js` collision. Persisting and playing a new geometry identity therefore needs a separate reviewed world/save migration; do not promote the offline-scoped digest as a playable save ID. Historical worlds must keep their original `world.js` geometry. Before any player or NPC uses this geometry, calibrated terrain grade, collision, sight, drawing, dry spawn and cave approaches must agree under a versioned fresh-world profile. Radius-aware route tests must establish real crossings and reachable corridors. Agent perception may receive only local qualitative observations through the existing perception boundary, never sampler fields or hidden ecology. The crest and drainage ownership are represented, but physical water and playable slope limits remain unresolved.
