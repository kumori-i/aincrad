# Brief grounded naming follow-ups

A successful live name introduction may open one eight-second runtime opportunity for the same speaker, listener and individual. During that opportunity the existing request, independent selection and independent confirmation can speak at least one second apart rather than waiting the ordinary 35-second unsolicited speech cooldown. Every turn updates ordinary speech timestamps. At most three follow-up utterances are possible; failed one-shot attempts do not retry.

This is bounded conversation timing, not mutual attention or new learning machinery. It cannot pause a body, move an agent, cancel physical work, give a skill/food reward or confirm a reference without the existing independent evidence. Ordinary controllers and body/world time continue unchanged. The opportunity ends on expiry, local/safety failure, lost current referent, food handover, agent replacement or disposal. It is not saved; restoring labels does not restore a continuation or replay a consumed request.

A preregistered controlled comparison used two actor seeds and two listener offsets for 10 seconds after a genuinely paid encounter and local grounding. Baseline and eight-second hold-only modes confirmed 0/4; one-second follow-up timing and timing-plus-voluntary-holds confirmed 4/4. Timing-only required no hold decisions and retained baseline movement endpoints in these four cases. Therefore this increment omits body holds entirely. These tiny staged encounters are not evidence of spontaneous language, generally improved survival or guaranteed future communication.

Regression coverage includes strict save/reload, urgent care without an available solution, lost range/LOS/referent, food priority, one-shot expiration, ordinary speech spacing, ongoing paid actions and unchanged motor evidence. The ordinary cooldown still short-circuits costly referent sensing outside a continuation.
